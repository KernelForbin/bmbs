Today's & Yesterday's Picks

  • The current slate stays live and tracked all the way through midnight — a 10pm game doesn't get cut off by the calendar flipping over.
  • Once the very last game from that slate finishes, it moves automatically to Yesterday's Picks with final results.
  • Today's Picks then waits for the next slate, and shows a queued heads-up if tomorrow's picks are already in but tonight isn't over yet.

Running payout estimate

  • See a running total payout at the top of the page, updating live as bets hit.
  • If a leg is voided for a no-show player, the payout recalculates automatically for the legs still in play.
  • Treat it as an estimate, not a settled figure — it's built from the odds on the picks as written, and your book has the final say (see Pinch Hit Protection below for one case where they can disagree).

Live tracking, no refreshing

  • Every leg updates on its own about every 10 seconds, straight from MLB's real game data — leave the tab open and watch it change.
  • The instant a home run shows up in the play-by-play, that leg turns green — even while the game is still going.
  • A player whose team never takes the field for them is marked "Didn't play" and doesn't count as a loss.

Live at-bat context

  • While a game's in progress, see the current inning and whether your guy is up now or coming up soon.
  • AT THE PLATE NOW and GUARANTEED TO BAT THIS INNING flag legs about to be decided.
  • An estimated at-bats-remaining count and rough odds of another trip to the plate, based on league-average outcomes.
  • One tap jumps straight to the live play-by-play on MLB Gameday.

Live At Bats

  • Tap Live At Bats to open a wall of tiles: every pick who's at the plate right now or guaranteed to bat this inning, across every game at once. Even closed, its header tells you how many are up and due.
  • The man at the plate gets a lit-up tile with the pitcher he's facing, the live count, outs, and a dot for every pitch so far — ball, called strike, swinging strike, foul, in play — plus the last pitch spelled out.
  • Behind him, in order: ON DECK, IN THE HOLE, then the hitters leading off or due up next half-inning. Each tile shows the odds and whose pick it is.
  • When the at-bat ends, the result holds the tile for a few seconds — STRIKEOUT, DOUBLE, whatever it was — then it drops off and everyone slides up. A home run turns the tile into a 💣 first, then shows the distance and exit velo.
  • A 🧇 marks an Iron, so you can see at a glance which at-bat could cash something. The scoreboard filters work here too: tap IRONS and only those hitters remain; tap a name in the Bettor Tracker and it's just that person's guys.
  • Only picks that can still win something appear — a hitter on a parlay that's already dead doesn't take up a tile. It refreshes with the rest of the page, so pitches arrive in small bursts rather than one by one. Tap any tile for Gameday.

Irons — one swing away

  • An Iron is any bet sitting exactly one home run from cashing: a parlay where every leg but one has hit, or any single that's still open.
  • A 🧇 waffle marks the exact player who still has to go deep, so you can spot what you're sweating at a glance. It's always visible — you don't have to switch filters to see it.
  • Tap the IRONS counter to see just those bets. Parlays show every leg, hits included, so you get the full picture of how close the card is.

Bomb alerts

  • When someone in the day's picks goes deep, the page announces it: "<Player> BOMB! 💣".
  • Two independent switches at the top — Overlay (a celebration right on the page) and Push (a normal phone/desktop notification, even if you're on another tab). Have either, both, or neither; the setting sticks on that device.
  • Push has to be switched on by you, and your browser will ask permission first. It works while the page is open somewhere — not once you've closed it entirely.
  • Opening the page mid-game won't bury you in alerts for home runs that already happened — you only get told about ones hit while you're watching.

Tap-to-filter scoreboard

  • Quick counts of where your bets stand — Open, Irons, Hit, Missed.
  • Tap any number to filter the whole page down to just that group — the tickets and the Live At Bats tiles alike. Tap it again to clear the filter.

Home Run Log

  • A tap-to-open log of every home run of the day, newest first — on both the Today and Yesterday tabs.
  • Switch between Our Picks and All Home Runs (the whole league). Either way, anyone in the group's picks is highlighted so you can spot the ones that matter.
  • Tap any home run for the full Statcast detail: exit velo, launch angle, distance, the pitch he hit and how hard it came in, where it was in the strike zone, plus ballpark, wind and temperature.

Bettor Tracker

  • A collapsible leaderboard showing each person's hits, misses, and their average + best hit odds for the slate.
  • Tap a name to filter the whole page to just that person's legs — including who shows up in Live At Bats.

History — the whole record

  • A separate History page (linked at the bottom of the live card) covers every slate the group has logged — refreshed daily from the tracking sheet.
  • Bettor leaderboard: legs, hits, hit rate, best hit, longest drought, and how you'd have done betting every pick straight. Tap a name to filter the whole page to that person.
  • Are the odds worth it? Hit rate by odds range against what those odds need just to break even, plus a slate-by-slate chart of how each night went.
  • Players: who gets picked most, who actually delivers, and who's picking them. Tap a player to see every parlay he was in.
  • Heartbreakers — parlays that missed by exactly one leg, and the parlay killers responsible — and The ones that got away: guys the group backs all the time who went deep on days nobody had them.
  • Every parlay ever logged, filterable by who, what happened, and which player.

Pinch Hit Protection

  • If your guy gets pulled for a pinch hitter, the bet isn't dead — most of our books cover it, so the site keeps tracking whoever took his spot in the lineup.
  • If that replacement goes deep, the leg is credited as a hit, counted in your payout and the Bettor Tracker like any other. It's marked with a green-and-yellow striped check so you can tell it came through protection rather than your guy's own bat.
  • Tracking follows the whole chain — if the pinch hitter is later replaced too, the next man up still counts.
  • Only if nobody in that lineup spot homers by the end of the game does it become a real miss, with a note saying so.
  • Check your own book on this one. Pinch hit protection isn't a league rule — it's a sportsbook policy, and the details vary: some books cover it, some don't, and the ones that do don't all handle it the same way. This site assumes it applies, so a striped leg is our best guess at how it'll settle, not a guarantee. That also means the payout figure at the top is an estimate, not a confirmed number.
