Today's & Yesterday's Picks

  • The current slate stays live and tracked all the way through midnight — a 10pm game doesn't get cut off by the calendar flipping over.
  • Once the very last game from that slate finishes, it moves automatically to Yesterday's Picks with final results.
  • Today's Picks then waits for the next slate, and shows a queued heads-up if tomorrow's picks are already in but tonight isn't over yet.

Running payout estimate

  • See a running total payout at the top of the page, updating live as bets hit.
  • If a leg is voided for a no-show player, the payout recalculates automatically for the legs still in play.
  • Treat it as an estimate, not a settled figure — it's built from the odds on the picks as written, and your book has the final say (see Pinch Hit Protection below for one case where they can disagree).

Live tracking, no refreshing

  • Every leg updates on its own about every 10 seconds, straight from MLB's real game data — leave the tab open and watch it change.
  • The instant a home run shows up in the play-by-play, that leg turns green — even while the game is still going.
  • A player whose team never takes the field for them is marked "Didn't play" and doesn't count as a loss.

Live at-bat context

  • While a game's in progress, see the current inning and whether your guy is up now or coming up soon.
  • AT THE PLATE NOW and GUARANTEED TO BAT THIS INNING flag legs about to be decided.
  • An estimated at-bats-remaining count and rough odds of another trip to the plate, based on league-average outcomes.
  • One tap jumps straight to the live play-by-play on MLB Gameday.

Stolen base bets

  • A pick isn't just home runs anymore — a leg can be a STEAL bet instead, and one parlay can mix both kinds. Everything else about it — payouts, filters, the Bettor Tracker — works exactly the same either way.
  • The instant a stolen base shows up in the play-by-play, that leg turns green — even mid at-bat, since a runner can swipe a bag before the batter's own turn is even over.
  • Steal odds can run negative (a burner on the bases is often the favorite) — the page always shows the sign, never garbles it into something like "+-120".
  • One difference from home run bets: a steal leg has no Pinch Hit Protection. If that player's pulled from the game before he's swiped a bag, the leg is a miss right away. A player who never got into the game at all is still marked "Didn't play" and voided.

Live Bet Tracker

  • Tap Live Bet Tracker to open a wall of tiles: every pick who's at the plate right now, on base, or guaranteed to bat this inning, across every game at once. Even closed, its header tells you how many are up, on base, and due.
  • The man at the plate gets a lit-up tile with the pitcher he's facing, the live count, outs, and a dot for every pitch so far — ball, called strike, swinging strike, foul, in play — plus the last pitch spelled out.
  • A steal pick who reaches base gets his own tile the moment he's on — ON 1ST, ON 2ND, or ON 3RD — showing the outs, who's up, and whether the next bag is open or blocked by another runner.
  • Behind them, in order: ON DECK, IN THE HOLE, then the hitters leading off or due up next half-inning. Each tile shows the odds and whose pick it is.
  • When the at-bat ends, the result holds the tile for a few seconds — STRIKEOUT, DOUBLE, whatever it was — then it drops off and everyone slides up. A home run turns the tile into a 💣 first, then shows the distance and exit velo. A steal turns it into a 🏃 first, then shows SAFE or CAUGHT — and if he's left on base with no attempt, a quick note says so instead of just vanishing.
  • A 🧇 marks an Iron, so you can see at a glance which at-bat could cash something. The scoreboard filters work here too: tap IRONS and only those hitters remain; tap a name in the Bettor Tracker and it's just that person's guys.
  • Only picks that can still win something appear — a hitter on a parlay that's already dead doesn't take up a tile. It refreshes with the rest of the page, so pitches arrive in small bursts rather than one by one. Tap any tile for Gameday.

Irons — one swing away

  • An Iron is any bet sitting exactly one home run from cashing: a parlay where every leg but one has hit, or any single that's still open.
  • A 🧇 waffle marks the exact player who still has to go deep, so you can spot what you're sweating at a glance. It's always visible — you don't have to switch filters to see it.
  • Tap the IRONS counter to see just those bets. Parlays show every leg, hits included, so you get the full picture of how close the card is.

Bomb & steal alerts

  • When someone in the day's picks goes deep, the page announces it: "<Player> BOMB! 💣". A stolen-base pick gets the same treatment when he swipes a bag: "<Player> STOLE 2ND! 🏃".
  • If that swing (or steal) cashes a bet — an Iron finishing off a parlay, or a single coming home — the same alert goes gold 💰: money rains down the screen and it tells you what hit and for how much, like "2-LEG PARLAY CASHED · $154.00". One play that cashes several bets adds them up. The push notification says it too.
  • No alert fires for a home run or steal that can't change anything — if every bet naming that player is a parlay some other leg already missed, the page still tracks the hit but doesn't interrupt you with a popup for it.
  • Two independent switches at the top — Overlay (a celebration right on the page) and Push (a normal phone/desktop notification, even if you're on another tab). Have either, both, or neither; the setting sticks on that device.
  • Push has to be switched on by you, and your browser will ask permission first. It works while the page is open somewhere — not once you've closed it entirely.
  • Opening the page mid-game won't bury you in alerts for home runs or steals that already happened — you only get told about ones that happen while you're watching.

Always know where a pick stands

  • Every pick now says what's happening to him, in plain words, at every stage — not just once his game is live. "Game hasn't started yet." before first pitch, "Game on — not in the lineup yet." if he's on the roster but not playing, "Game final — no home run." once it's over.
  • If his game is held up you get the reason rather than a blank: "Game delayed — rain.", "Game suspended.", or "Warming up — first pitch shortly." while the lineups are out but nothing has been thrown. Nobody is shown as being at the plate until the game actually starts.
  • A player who sat on the bench all game is now correctly marked N/A — void / refunded rather than counted as a miss. That's how the books treat it, and it's what the History page has always recorded; the live page now agrees with both.

Parlay Cards & Straight Bet Cards

  • The bets themselves now sit in two collapsible sections, the same way the panels above them do — Parlay Cards for the multi-leg cards, Straight Bet Cards for the one-player bets.
  • Both start closed, so the page opens on the summary instead of a wall of tickets. Each header carries its own tally — 3 OPEN · 1 HIT · 2 MISSED — so you can read where each group stands without opening it.
  • Whichever one you open stays open on that device, and stays open while the page keeps refreshing.

Tap-to-filter scoreboard

  • Quick counts of where your bets stand — Open, Irons, Hit, Missed.
  • Underneath, a second row counts the individual legs — Live, Hit, Missed, N/A — so you can see how the slate is really going, not just how many cards are still alive. Both rows are the same four states in the same order and the same colours, so reading straight down a column compares like with like.
  • A bet lands in N/A when it's been refunded — every leg on the card was a player who didn't play, or a straight bet on someone who never got in. Those used to sit in Open, which made it look like you still had something riding on them.
  • Irons has its own slim amber bar just under the bets. It isn't a fifth state — an Iron is already counted in Open — so it sits apart from the four rather than competing with them. Tap it to filter, same as any chip.
  • Tap any number to filter the whole page down to just that group — the tickets and the Live Bet Tracker tiles alike. A leg filter narrows what's shown inside a card too, and says how many legs it hid.
  • Pick as many as you like. The filters no longer replace each other: tap Open and Hit to see both, add Legs: Live and Missed on top, then tap two names in the Bettor Tracker — all at once. Within a row the choices add up; across rows they narrow, so "Open bets, missed legs, Kenny" means exactly that.
  • A Filters bar shows everything you've picked, one removable tag each, with a Clear all button. It follows you down the page, so you can drop a filter from wherever you happen to be reading.
  • Expand all / Collapse all sit just above the Live Drives and open or close every panel at once — Live Drives, Touchdown Log, Bettor Tracker, Parlay Cards and Straight Bet Cards.
  • Expand all / Collapse all sit just above the Live Bet Tracker and open or close every panel at once — Live Bet Tracker, Home Run Log, Bettor Tracker, Parlay Cards and Straight Bet Cards.

Home Run Log

  • A tap-to-open log of every home run of the day, newest first — on both the Today and Yesterday tabs. Its header shows a running 3 OURS · 12 TOTAL · 25% count — how many of the day's home runs were yours — so you can gauge the action without opening it.
  • Switch between Our Picks and All Home Runs (the whole league). Either way, anyone in the group's picks is highlighted so you can spot the ones that matter.
  • Tap any home run for the full Statcast detail: exit velo, launch angle, distance, the pitch he hit and how hard it came in, where it was in the strike zone, plus ballpark, wind and temperature.

Bettor Tracker

  • A collapsible leaderboard showing each person's hits, misses, and their average + best hit odds for the slate. Its header counts how many people are in on the slate. Now that steal bets can run at negative odds, a favorite like -120 always shows its sign correctly, never garbled into something like "+-120".
  • Tap a name to filter the whole page to just that person's legs — including who shows up in the Live Bet Tracker.

History — the whole record

  • A separate History page (linked at the bottom of the live card) covers every slate the group has logged, and updates itself automatically each morning.
  • Real money: a tile showing actual dollars staked vs. returned across the slates the tracker has recorded, not just a hypothetical.
  • Bettor leaderboard: legs, hits, hit rate, best hit, longest drought, and how you'd have done betting every pick straight. Tap a name to filter the whole page to that person.
  • Are the odds worth it? Hit rate by odds range against what those odds need just to break even, plus a slate-by-slate chart of how each night went.
  • Players: who gets picked most, who actually delivers, and who's picking them. Tap a player to see every parlay he was in.
  • Heartbreakers — parlays that missed by exactly one leg, and the parlay killers responsible — and The ones that got away: guys the group backs all the time who went deep on days nobody had them.
  • Every parlay ever logged, filterable by who, what happened, and which player.

Pinch Hit Protection

  • If your guy gets pulled for a pinch hitter, the bet isn't dead — most of our books cover it, so the site keeps tracking whoever took his spot in the lineup.
  • If that replacement goes deep, the leg is credited as a hit, counted in your payout and the Bettor Tracker like any other. It's marked with a green-and-yellow striped check so you can tell it came through protection rather than your guy's own bat.
  • Tracking follows the whole chain — if the pinch hitter is later replaced too, the next man up still counts.
  • Only if nobody in that lineup spot homers by the end of the game does it become a real miss, with a note saying so.
  • Check your own book on this one. Pinch hit protection isn't a league rule — it's a sportsbook policy, and the details vary: some books cover it, some don't, and the ones that do don't all handle it the same way. This site assumes it applies, so a striped leg is our best guess at how it'll settle, not a guarantee. That also means the payout figure at the top is an estimate, not a confirmed number.

This Week's & Last Week's Picks

  • Football runs by the NFL week, not the calendar day — a card can go up days ahead (Saturday for Sunday, or earlier for a Thursday-night pick) and cover Thursday, Sunday and Monday games all at once.
  • This Week's Picks stays put through the whole week, even if every pick on the card plays Sunday — it only moves once the week's very last game (usually Monday night) goes final.
  • Once it moves, it lands in Last Week's Picks with final results, and This Week waits for the next card — showing a queued heads-up if it's already uploaded but the week isn't over yet.

  • A player who was inactive for the game is marked "Didn't play" and doesn't count as a loss — but a player who dressed and simply never found the end zone is a real miss.

Live game context

  • While a game's in progress, see the score, the clock, and his stat line so far right under the pick.
  • IN THE RED ZONE and ON OFFENSE flag legs whose offense is on the field right now, closest to paying off. A quieter TAKING THE FIELD SOON means his side has just won the ball back but hasn't snapped it yet — green is reserved for when he can actually score on the next play.
  • Once his game is final, the live line is replaced with his final stat line.
  • One tap jumps straight to the live play-by-play on ESPN.

  • When a drive ends, the result holds the tile for a few seconds — PUNT, FIELD GOAL, or TD — NOT HIM if a teammate scored instead — then it drops off and everyone slides up.
  • When your own pick scores, his tile turns into a 🏈 first, then shows the play — how many yards, and how it happened.
